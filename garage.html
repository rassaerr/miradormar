Garage & Gate Access

Nice Spin motor operation, emergency manual override, visual guide, and key fob programming.

1. Motor System & Access Architecture

The main vehicular access to the underground parking garage (-1 level) is powered by a ceiling-mounted Nice Spin electromechanical motor running along an overhead guide rail.

When a key fob button is pressed, an encrypted radio signal is transmitted to the central control receiver located inside the locked communal plant room. The overhead motor unit handles mechanical lifting and lowering, while access permissions and authorization codes are stored inside the central receiver unit.

2. Emergency Manual Override Instructions

In the event of a power outage, electrical fault, or system maintenance, follow these exact procedure steps to operate the garage gate manually:

Step 1: Check the Frame Lock Bolt

Inspect the internal frame of the door panel (see Photo 1). Ensure the manual steel slide bolt is fully retracted (unlocked). If engaged into the track, the gate frame remains mechanically locked in place.

Step 2: Disengage the Motor Drive

Locate the black release ball hanging from the overhead carriage track (see Photo 2). Pull the release ball straight downward firmly until you hear a clear mechanical click, uncoupling the door carriage from the motor belt.

CRITICAL WARNING

Pull the release cord straight downward only. Never apply horizontal force or attempt to drag the gate using the cord, as sideways strain will shear the internal release lever mechanism.

Step 3: Lift or Lower the Gate

Grasp the central metal handle located on the lower door panel (see Photo 1) to manually lift or lower the gate along its vertical side tracks.

Step 4: Re-engage Automatic Mode

When electrical power is restored, pull the release cord back toward the motor unit or actuate your key fob. The carriage will automatically latch back onto the drive belt during its next movement cycle.

3. Key Fobs, Maintenance & Authorization

Official Key Fob Models

The complex gate system is compatible with two official Nice radio transmitter models:

Nice Flor-S: Classic rectangular dark blue or black transmitter with 2 control buttons.

Nice ERA INTI: Modern compact square transmitter.

Battery Replacement Procedure

If the transmitter LED appears dim or fails to illuminate upon button press, replace the internal coin cell battery:

  • Carefully pry open the plastic casing using a coin or small flathead precision tool along the side seam.
  • Remove the depleted coin cell battery (Type CR2032 for ERA INTI or Type CR2016 for Flor-S).
  • Insert a fresh 3V coin battery with the positive (+) terminal facing upward, then snap the casing halves firmly together.

Troubleshooting Signal Issues

Red LED flashes, but the gate does not respond?

If the red LED lights up upon press but the gate remains stationary (even after installing a new battery), the transmitter has lost synchronization or its digital serial number was cleared from receiver memory. The key fob must be re-authorized.

Ordering & Authorization Procedure

Purchasing Replacement Transmitters

Compatible replacement key fobs can be purchased directly from online retailers (e.g., Amazon):

  • Official Nice Transmitters: Nice ERA INTI (2-Channel) or Nice Flor-S (Approx. €18 – €25).
  • Generic Transmitters: 433.92 MHz rolling-code remotes specified as Nice Flor-S compatible (Approx. €8 – €12).

Registering a New Key Fob

A newly purchased key fob will not operate the gate out of the box due to rolling-code security encryption. Every new transmitter must be digitally registered into the central receiver memory.

System Access: Contact Francisca to request access authorization and coordination for adding new transmitter codes.

Administrative Fees: Consult building management regarding applicable registration or administrative processing fees.

Authorization Mechanics

The central receiver extracts the transmitter's unique factory digital ID and writes it directly into the memory register of the Mirador del Mar gate receiver, enabling instantaneous recognition.

4. Technical Overview (Nice & Rolling Code System)

Summary of the radio communication protocol and security specifications powering the garage gate system:

  • Operating Frequency: Standardized 433.92 MHz radio frequency band.
  • Rolling Code Security: Each transmission sends an encrypted, single-use code calculated via a rolling mathematical counter, preventing code interception and signal replay attacks.
  • Memory Registration: Due to rolling-code security protocols, transmitters cannot be cloned over-the-air and must be explicitly paired into the central receiver memory database.
